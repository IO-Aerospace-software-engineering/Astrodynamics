# Covariance Propagation Provenance

This page traces every equation implemented for the state transition matrix and covariance work (phase 2,
feature 1), including the frame-change velocities that the covariance work relies on. For each one it gives the quantity, where it is implemented, the published reference it comes from,
the tests that check it, and the status of the citation:

- **Verified on the text**: the cited passage was read and matches the implementation.
- **To be verified by S. Guillet**: the reference could not be read from the development environment. The
  implementation is checked by the listed tests, and the citation awaits confirmation on the text.
- **Confirmed by S. Guillet**: a citation of the previous kind that S. Guillet has confirmed, with the date.

Rows are added by each pull request that implements an equation. A formula of the new code that is missing from
this page is a defect.

## Finite-Difference Steps

The relative steps of the default central differences come from a plateau study: the error of the default path
against a reference, for relative steps from 1e-12 to 1e-2 (half decades), for every built-in force at the four
reference states (LEO 400 km, LEO 800 km, GEO, perigee of a 500 × 40 000 km HEO; 2021-03-20 12:00 UTC, all in
sunlight). The reference is analytic for the point mass and the third bodies, and Ridders' extrapolation for the
others. The test `FiniteDifferenceStepStudyTests` reruns it and writes the curves when the environment variable
`IO_ASTRODYNAMICS_STUDY_OUTPUT` names a directory.

Two errors are measured: relative to the block of the force itself, and, for `∂a/∂r`, scaled by the point-mass
block of the central body, which is the error the state transition matrix sees once the blocks of all the forces are
summed. They differ for SRP: its position derivative is about `|a| / d_sun`, so a step scaled by the geocentric `|r|`
leaves a rounding error near 1e-6 relative, but near 1e-17 scaled. Since step 5b, SRP and thermal pressure have
analytic partials, which are the reference of their rows; drag and the geopotential have their own partials, tested
against Ridders' reference, and the study measures the default path, which every force keeps.

difference. The test asserts, at the default steps, a relative error below 1e-4 (the threshold of the specification
for the default path), a scaled error of `∂a/∂r` and a relative error of `∂a/∂v` below 1e-9, and a Ridders error
estimate below 1e-7. These bounds are checked on the four states that chose the step, so the check is in-sample: it
guards the plateau against a change of a force, not the choice of the step itself. All four states are in sunlight;
the penumbra, where the shadow function makes the SRP partials large and only continuous at its edges, is measured by
`RadiationPressurePartialsTests` (step 5b, see the [assumptions](#modeling-assumptions)) and in the validation of the
state transition matrix (F1). Albedo and thermal pressure have no shadow.

### Density Gradient Step

The steps of the gradient in geodetic coordinates, `δ_h = 3e-6` (fourth order in altitude) and `δ_H = 3e-5` (along the
meridian and the parallel), relative to `|r|`, come from the plateau study of [#420](https://github.com/IO-Aerospace-software-engineering/Astrodynamics/issues/420)
(`DensityGradientStepStudyTests.GeodeticSteps_LieOnThePlateauOfTheDensityGradient`): the error of `∇ρ` against Ridders'
derivative of the density chain, which resolves it to 1.4e-12 to 1.6e-12, with NRLMSISE-00 at LEO 400 km, LEO 800 km and
the HEO perigee. At the chosen steps, 1.5e-12, 8.2e-12 and 2.3e-12; from `δ_h` 3e-6 to 1e-5 and `δ_H` 3e-6 to 3e-5,
6.0e-13 to 8.2e-12; at `δ_h = 1e-4` the truncation of the scale height leaves 2.5e-10 to 9.8e-10, at `δ_H = 3e-7` the
rounding of the model 1.7e-11 to 8.8e-11. The horizontal step can be ten times that of the altitude because the density
varies horizontally over thousands of kilometres.

Data: [density-geodetic-step-study.csv](../assets/covariance/density-geodetic-step-study.csv) (Linux, .NET 10,
2026-10-21).

The relative step `δ_ρ` of the Cartesian differences of the density, kept within `δ_H` of the polar axis and for the
rules across the cutoffs, comes from its own plateau study,
`DensityGradientStepStudyTests`: the error of `∇ρ` against Ridders' derivative of the density chain, and of the drag
`∂a/∂r` against Ridders' derivative of the whole acceleration, for relative steps from 1e-12 to 1e-2 (half decades),
with NRLMSISE-00 at LEO 400 km, LEO 800 km and the HEO perigee. The step of the default path, 3e-6, scaled by `|r|`,
leaves 1.7e-8 on `∇ρ`: the density varies on its scale height, some 60 km, not on `|r|`.

![Error of the density gradient against the relative step](../assets/covariance/density-step-study.png)

Data: [density-step-study.csv](../assets/covariance/density-step-study.csv) (Linux, .NET 10, 2026-10-12).

The error falls as `δ²` (truncation over the scale height) down to 3e-7 and rises as `1/δ` (rounding of the model)
below: worst over the three states below 4e-9 from 1e-7 to 1e-6, 2.6e-10 to 5.6e-10 at the default `δ_ρ = 3e-7`
(`h ≈ 2 m` in LEO), the same for `∂a/∂r`, whose gradient term dominates. The test asserts, at the default step, a
relative error of both below 1e-8, and a Ridders error estimate below 1e-8 (measured 1.8e-12 to 3.6e-12); in-sample,
as the study above.

## Structural Properties (F2)

`StructuralPropertiesTests` checks the state transition matrix integrated by RK7(8) on the reference cases R1 to R5
of the specification (`ReferenceCases`, 2021-03-20 12:00 UTC), without drag and SRP, so that the dynamics are
conservative. `Φ` is measured in canonical units, with the initial radius and the matching time unit, at the end of
the arc: one day, two revolutions for R5. The values below are from Linux, .NET 10, 2026-10-08, and each one is written
by a committed test to its output; replayed on 2026-10-10 with the same values, identical in Debug and Release.

On R1 (two-body, LEO 700 km), every defect decreases with the tolerance, so it is the error of the integration
(`R1_DefectsDecreaseWithTheTolerance`, which asserts the decrease):

| Tolerance | `‖ΦᵀJΦ - J‖` | `|det Φ - 1|` | `Φ` against the Keplerian STM (worst 3×3 block) |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| 1e-9 | 3.0e-7 | 3.8e-8 | 1.2e-7 |
| 1e-11 | 2.8e-9 | 2.6e-10 | 9.9e-10 |
| 1e-13 | 2.1e-10 | 4.8e-11 | 8.2e-12 |

The tests run at 1e-13, the tolerance of the reference propagations of F1, and assert the thresholds of the
specification: below 1e-9 for the symplectic defect, the determinant and the Keplerian error (also checked at a
quarter and half of the day: 6.3e-13 and 2.2e-12).

R1 runs at an almost constant step, with `‖Φ‖` of order 1e2. `R5TwoBody_PhiMatchesTheKeplerianStm` compares `Φ` to
the Keplerian STM on the R5 state around a point-mass Earth, without third bodies, where the step changes by orders of
magnitude between perigee and apogee and `‖Φ‖` reaches 2.7e3: 2.7e-14, 7.8e-13, 2.4e-12 and 3.5e-12 at the first
apogee, the first perigee, the second apogee and the end of the two revolutions. The test asserts 1e-11, about three
times the largest value (Linux, .NET 10, 2026-10-10).

Until step 5a the geopotential partials were central differences, asymmetric by 2.1e-11 (R2) and 5.6e-11 (R5) of
`G`, which dominated the symplectic defect of the cases with a geopotential. The analytic partials of B3 are symmetric
by construction, and `ConservativeGeopotentialCases_PhiIsSymplecticWithUnitDeterminant` now tests the cases with
